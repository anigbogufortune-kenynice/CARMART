# Architectural Decision Records

> Generated by /write-specs from the Grill Me session (`issues/grill-notes.md`) and the PRD. Q-numbers refer to the Grill Me decisions.
> From Q9 onward the product owner delegated decisions ("use your recommendations"). ADR-006 to ADR-010 were made while writing the specs, under the same delegation.

---

## ADR-001: Listing marketplace; CarMart never handles money in v1

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q1, Q5

**Context:** Car sales involve inspection, test drives, ownership transfer and large sums.
**Decision:** Buyers contact shops in the app, and payment and handover happen offline. No payments, deposits or subscriptions in v1. `shops.plan` exists (default `free`) so paid plans can be added later.
**Rationale:** Avoids escrow, refunds, chargebacks and possible payments licensing, and keeps v1 focused on trust and image integrity.
**Consequences:** No revenue in v1, and CarMart carries the photo-check costs, capped by listing and upload limits (ADR-008).

## ADR-002: Verified shops: phone OTP plus admin approval, one shop per account

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q2

**Context:** Car marketplaces attract scam sellers.
**Decision:** Anyone can create a shop and draft listings. The shop becomes public only after an Australian mobile is verified by SMS OTP and an admin approves it. `shops.owner_id` is unique.
**Rationale:** Blocks throwaway scam accounts cheaply without excluding private sellers.
**Consequences:** An admin role and approval queue are needed from day one. SMS costs (Twilio) apply. A multi-shop or team model would need a migration later.

## ADR-003: Image integrity: two automated checks, three outcomes, human review; no 100% claim

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q3

**Context:** AI-image detectors give probabilities, not certainty, and EXIF metadata is unreliable in both directions.
**Decision:** Every photo gets a car check and an AI-generation check, and lands in `passed`, `rejected` or `in_review`. A listing goes live only when all its photos have passed. Metadata signals are recorded for reviewers but never decide on their own. Product wording says every photo is checked, and suspicious photos are blocked or reviewed. It never claims AI images are impossible.
**Rationale:** Honest, enforceable protection. The review queue absorbs the uncertainty.
**Consequences:** Admin workload scales with uncertain photos, and the thresholds (ADR-009) are tunable without a deploy.

## ADR-004: Separate, swappable providers for each check

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q4

**Context:** General-purpose object labels are weak at car-specific questions: interior versus exterior, toy or render, a photo of a screen.
**Decision:** The car check uses the Claude vision model with a strict JSON output schema. The AI check uses Sightengine (`models=genai`); a Hive fallback is deferred (ADR-013). Providers implement one interface and are chosen by env vars. The `fake` provider is mandatory in tests and local development.
**Rationale:** Best-in-class for each question, with no vendor lock-in, and CI never spends money.
**Consequences:** Two vendor accounts and two per-image costs. Vendor pricing and accuracy are confirmed at sign-up.

## ADR-005: Australia-only launch, with the market stored as data

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q6

**Decision:** AUD, km, `+61` mobiles, AU states and postcodes, the Australian Privacy Act, and Sydney-region hosting. `country` and `currency` columns on shops and listings instead of constants.
**Consequences:** Adding a country later is configuration plus localisation, not a schema rewrite.

## ADR-006: Background jobs run in a Node.js job runner behind internal routes; the service role is confined there

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** spec writing (refines PRD Q16, which named a Supabase Edge Function)

**Context:** The pipeline needs `sharp` (decode, pHash, metadata strip, WebP encode) on photos of up to 10 MB. Supabase Edge Functions run on Deno with tight CPU-time limits and can't use `sharp`. Email dispatch and scheduled work also need privileged database access.
**Decision:**
- All background work runs in `src/server/jobs/**`, exposed only through `src/app/api/internal/*` route handlers (Node runtime), authenticated with `INTERNAL_JOB_SECRET`.
- Postgres calls them through `pg_net`, from `AFTER INSERT` triggers and `pg_cron` safety-net schedules.
- `src/server/jobs/supabase-admin.ts` is the **only** place a service-role client is created. ESLint `no-restricted-imports` plus a test forbid importing it anywhere else.
- The PRD's `supabase/functions/process-image-checks` is replaced by `src/server/jobs/image-verification/`, and the PRD's `src/services/image-verification/*` providers move there too.

**Rationale:** Reliable image processing, one language and toolchain, and a single, auditable place with elevated privileges.
**Consequences:** CLAUDE.md's "no service role in app code" rule has this one documented exception. Internal routes must stay idempotent, because `pg_net` may retry.

## ADR-007: HEIC photos are converted to JPEG in the browser

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** spec writing (refines PRD AC-19)

**Context:** iPhones produce HEIC, but `sharp`'s prebuilt binaries don't decode HEIC (patent-encumbered codec).
**Decision:** The uploader converts HEIC/HEIF to JPEG client-side (the `heic2any` library) before requesting an upload URL. The server accepts only JPEG, PNG and WebP.
**Consequences:** Sellers can still pick HEIC photos (AC-19 behaviour holds from the user's point of view). The conversion adds a second or two per photo on the device.

## ADR-008: Free tier guardrails

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q5

**Decision:** 10 active listings per shop (admin-adjustable per shop), 60 photo uploads per shop per rolling 24 h, and 4–20 photos per listing. Also 20 new conversations per buyer per 24 h, and 10 reports per user per 24 h (Q19 rate limits).
**Consequences:** Predictable vendor spend and less abuse. All values live in `app_settings`.

## ADR-009: Starting image-decision thresholds

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted (to be tuned before launch) · **Source:** Q16 + spec writing

**Decision:**

| Signal | Rule | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| AI score | ≥ 0.90 | rejected |
| AI score | 0.50–0.89 | in_review |
| Car check | `is_car = false`, confidence ≥ 0.85 | rejected |
| Car check | `is_car = false` below 0.85, or `is_car = true` below 0.80 | in_review |
| Car check | `is_screen_or_print = true` | in_review |
| pHash | Hamming distance ≤ 6 to a photo from a **different** shop | in_review |
| Vendor error | after 3 attempts | in_review |

The worst outcome wins: rejected > in_review > passed.
**Rationale:** Conservative rejection (only when confident) and generous review.
**Consequences:** Pre-launch calibration on real Australian listing photos and known AI images is a Phase 5 QA task.

## ADR-010: Email through an outbox table

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** spec writing (refines PRD `notification_log`)

**Decision:** Every email is enqueued into `notifications` by `enqueue_notification()` (SQL) or `NotificationService.enqueue()`. One dispatcher sends through Resend, with retries. De-duplication and throttling (15 minutes per conversation) happen at enqueue time. This replaces the PRD's `notification_log` table.
**Consequences:** State changes and emails commit in the same transaction, so there's never an "approved but no email" gap. Emails are delayed by up to a minute.

## ADR-011: Status transitions only through `security definer` RPCs

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q8, Q19

**Decision:** Clients can update data fields under RLS, but `status` changes on shops, listings and images happen only in SQL functions that validate the transition table in `docs/systems/*`, increment `version`, write the audit rows and enqueue notifications atomically.
**Consequences:** The state machine can't be bypassed from the client. Transition logic is tested at both the service and SQL level.

## ADR-012: Hosting in Australia

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** Q19

**Decision:** Supabase production and staging in `ap-southeast-2` (Sydney). Netlify functions pinned to `syd` on the Pro plan (ADR-014; originally Vercel `syd1`).
**Consequences:** Low latency for Australian users, and data stays onshore.

## ADR-013: Hive backup provider deferred

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** spec writing (refines ADR-004)

**Context:** Hive's public API documentation couldn't be confirmed while writing the specs, and a second AI vendor isn't needed to launch.
**Decision:** v1 ships the `AiCheckProvider` interface with Sightengine and the fake provider only. Hive is added later as one provider file, when a fallback is actually needed.
**Consequences:** If Sightengine is down, photos go to `in_review` after 3 attempts (D9) instead of failing over automatically.

## ADR-014: Host the app on Netlify

**Date:** 2026-10-03 · **Status:** accepted · **Source:** owner request (supersedes the Vercel choice in ADR-012)

**Context:** The owner wants CarMart on Netlify. Netlify runs Next.js 14 through its OpenNext adapter with no configuration, and allows 60 s per synchronous request. Its Next.js guide lists `sharp` (a native module) as unsupported inside the Next.js server, while plain Netlify Functions can ship it with `external_node_modules`. The photo pipeline depends on `sharp`.
**Decision:** The Next.js app (pages, `/api/*`, middleware) deploys to Netlify as-is. Photo checks run in the Netlify **background function** `netlify/functions/process-image-checks.mts` (up to 15 minutes; it drains the queue). Postgres finds it through the Vault setting `image_check_path` (`/.netlify/functions/process-image-checks`), set with `configure_internal_jobs(base_url, secret, image_check_path)`. Without that setting Postgres calls the Next.js route `/api/internal/process-image-checks`, which local stacks and CI keep using. Email dispatch and unpublishing stay as Next.js routes (no native modules, well under 60 s).
**Consequences:** One extra entry point for the same pipeline code (`src/server/jobs/image-verification/background.ts`). Functions run in US East (Ohio) unless the site is on Netlify Pro with the region set to Sydney (`syd`), which matters because every request talks to the Sydney database. The first production deploy must be checked by uploading a photo and watching it reach `passed`.
